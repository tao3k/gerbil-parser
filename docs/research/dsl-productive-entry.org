* Productivity: finite derivation and LR entry admission

The shared =compute-productive= analysis and =validate-resolved-start= consumer
are implemented in =src/compiler/lr.ss=. LR compilation checks the selected start
before FIRST/state construction. This is an intentional admission change for an
entry with no finite derivation. The domain is the existing
lowered production graph after POO selection. Selected declaration origins must
remain available to the diagnostic consumer.

For a nonterminal A, =productive(A)= means that A has at least one finite
derivation to terminal symbols, possibly epsilon. The equations are:

#+begin_example
productive(terminal) = true
productive(empty rhs) = true
productive(rhs sequence) = every rhs operand is productive
productive(A) = some production for A has a productive rhs
nullable(A) implies productive(A)
productive(A) does not imply nullable(A)
#+end_example

The least fixed point starts with no productive nonterminals. Pure cycles add
no fact. Terminal occurrences satisfy productivity obligations, whereas they
block nullable clauses. Thus the current occurrence propagation is a relevant
implementation pattern, but its terminal policy cannot be reused unchanged.
A private graph-sharing abstraction requires actual consumers and measured
benefit; do not add an author callback or a new language-level analyzer slot.

Examples establish the necessary distinctions:

| Effective rules | Nullable | Productive | FIRST alone sufficient? |
|-----------------+----------+------------+-------------------------|
| A -> A | false | false | No finite exit exists |
| A -> epsilon | true | true | Empty FIRST can still have a finite derivation |
| A -> A a or b | false | true | Ordinary productive LR left recursion is valid |
| A -> a B; B -> B | false | false | FIRST can contain a even though no derivation completes |

** Target policy and paper boundary

For every existing =compile-lr-spec= route and conflict policy, an unproductive
selected root is an author error: =LR start rule has no finite terminal derivation=.
The lowered grammar is analyzed after POO selection and repetition validation;
no additional author slot or schema version is introduced. A productive root is
not rejected by productivity because of an unreachable dead rule or a dead alternative.
Independent conflict obligations remain: a nullable optional around a pure unit
cycle can still have a reduce/reduce conflict despite its epsilon exit. Neither
the lowered productions nor public LRSpec rows are pruned or changed. Existing
concise-language missing-reference validation still owns that distinct error.

This target policy is our design choice, not a theorem or requirement from
[[https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~nk480/parsing.pdf][Krishnaswami and Yallop, section 3]]. Their algebra includes the empty language
and distinguishes it from the singleton empty word. We preserve that distinction
in the analysis while requiring a usable finite entry for the current LR target.
The paper's predictive typing/linear parsing theorem is not extended to our LR
routes. Terminal productivity is over abstract grammar symbols: it does not
prove lexer realizability, successful precedence resolution, semantic action
termination, tree equivalence or a particular input's acceptance.

** Occurrence worklist and explanation

Each lowered production has one remaining count for its nonterminal occurrences.
Terminals satisfy their obligations immediately. A clause with no remaining
nonterminals seeds its lhs, including epsilon clauses. Register every reference
occurrence, including duplicates, before draining work. Each productive name
publishes once and discharges each registered occurrence once. A clause that
reaches zero publishes its lhs; alternative productions share that membership
fact. A cycle without a finite exit and undefined references cannot seed facts.
The result list is materialized in first-lhs order after convergence.

Soundness follows by induction on publication: seed clauses derive finite words;
a clause whose children are already productive can concatenate their finite
words. Completeness follows by induction on the height of any finite derivation:
its terminal/epsilon leaves seed facts, then its productions' occurrences are
discharged. Expected constant-time table operations give O(P + R) preparation
and propagation for P productions and R rhs occurrences, with O(P + R) storage.
This bound is for grammar analysis, not whole-parser execution.

The error carries =startOrigin= and a finite =unproductiveRuleBlockers= graph.
Origins use selected rule metadata and expression paths. Every choice alternative
has a blocker; one unproductive operand suffices for a sequence. Reference edges
identify the authored occurrence and target rule. Each rule is expanded once,
so cycles terminate without inventing source coordinates or exposing synthetic
helper names. This graph explains the completed fixed point; a single observed
cycle or bounded failure to enumerate a word is not the proof of unproductivity.

* Scheme author example and the two design questions

#+begin_src scheme
;; Existing grammar.ss syntax: recursion is language intent, not an engine loop.
(deflanguage finite-chain
  (syntax
    (lexical
      (root source-file)
      (lex (punctuation Punctuation (literals "a" "b"))
           (unknown Unknown (fallback)))
      (extras) (keywords) (recoveries)
      (conflicts reject) (case-insensitive #f)))
  (rules
    (source-file (node SourceFile expression))
    (expression (choice (seq expression (literal "a")) (literal "b")))))

;; Question 1: Why is this DSL design better?
;; The author writes recursive syntax once. Hygienic Scheme vocabulary and POO
;; selection feed one engine-owned analysis; changing the effective finite exit
;; changes admission without an author-maintained analyzer or callback.
;; Question 2: Why does this DSL design have a scientific basis?
;; Finite derivation has a precise meaning and least-fixed-point equations.
;; Independent oracles, legal LR recursion and selected-origin controls can
;; falsify the implementation. The admission policy is stated separately from
;; the papers' semantic and predictive-parser claims.
;; Limit: these controls establish this contract, not universal DSL superiority.
#+end_src

** Semantic domain and efficient evaluation

For each defined nonterminal, the private two-bit state has only three semantic
values: unknown, productive-only, and nullable-and-productive. A finite empty
word is also a finite terminal word, so nullable admission publishes both bits.
This invariant does not let productivity publish nullable: a terminal-only exit
is a direct counterexample. References without a defining lhs retain obligations
with no publisher; marked operands are interpreted through =base-symbol=.

A private =completion-clause= holds the lhs index and two remaining occurrence
counts. A terminal disables its nullable counter but satisfies its productive
obligation. Every nonterminal occurrence is counted, including duplicates.
Dense lhs indices, vector dependencies/facts/pending deltas and one worklist
share discovery and scheduling. All dependencies exist before the seed queue
drains. A queued index accumulates new bits; popping clears its pending delta
and decrements only the counters corresponding to those new facts.

Each domain's equation is unchanged. Soundness of combined nullable publication
follows from nullable implies productive; both derivations concatenate already
justified finite child derivations. Completeness follows from finite derivation
height as in the separate solvers. Queue exhaustion satisfies both equations,
and starting with no facts yields their least solutions. Shared scheduling and
bit coalescing therefore change evaluation work, not the completed facts.
Each name can publish at most two bits and each registered reference occurrence
is visited at most twice. With expected constant-time index operations, graph
preparation/propagation and storage are O(P + R); public list/table publication
is O(N). One graph is shared, not one undifferentiated Boolean fact.

[[https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~nk480/parsing.pdf][Krishnaswami and Yallop, section 3]] supplies the language/fixed-point reading.
The finite joint domain and dependency schedule above are this engine's design;
the paper's predictive grammar typing and linear parsing theorem are not claims
about our LR routes. This refinement does not prove lexical realizability,
semantic-action termination or general runtime speed.

** Controls and receipt boundary

The independent repeated-scan nullable/productivity oracles both observe the
joint result across the existing 729 cyclic combinations and eight real LR
fixture families. New controls distinguish productive-before-nullable from
coalesced two-bit publication by reversing seed order. They include duplicate
operands, missing references, terminal-blocked clauses and the empty graph.
Existing finite exits, productive LR recursion, dead-start admission, selected
POO origins and conflict/repetition diagnostics retain their independent controls.
