#+title: Parser engine convergence plan
#+startup: overview

* Current baseline and evidence

Reviewed remote baseline: PR #13 =007bf80=. Rust CI passed; both native jobs
failed because conformance preparation included compiler-generated memo
products in its immutable input inventory. The current change corrects that
ownership and adds native Scheme controls. The FFI/schema convergence and
Source cost implementation have local Scheme, C and Rust receipts; their
complete delivered-head CI remains required. Historical receipts below are
not current CI evidence.

The eight language families use the three author interfaces: =grammar.ss=
(syntax/rules), =parser.ss= (metadata and admitted execution), and
=parser-test.ss= (fixtures and conformance). Ten loader scopes are admitted.
Source lexical edit histories exist in Scheme, standalone Rust and native FFI.
They do not establish Source command/CST subtree reuse: recognition and
canonical publication still execute for every edit.

* Active sequence and exit gates

| Order | Complete change unit | Exit gate |
|-------+----------------------+-----------|
| 1 | FFI ownership and schema convergence | One handle-based parse API, no builtin name-dispatch facade, no versioned file/namespace names, Schema-owned ABI identities, migrated real consumers |
| 2 | Source edit cost attribution | Actual C calls, fresh/session full GPA1 parity, uninstrumented matched samples, separate request-local phase observations, retained-data counts and bounded memory claims |
| 3 | Measured engine refactor | Select the dominant complete-call cost; exact semantic controls and matched performance/space evidence; reject unstable or negative candidates |
| 4 | Strategy and language convergence | Audit all ten loaders, remove language-specific engine branches through admitted strategy data where justified, unsupported targets reject explicitly |
| 5 | Downstream and delivery | Reproducible source generation, independent Rust/C/Scheme consumer gates, clean native builds and exact-head CI |

Steps 1 and 2 are the current implementation unit. Step 3 is selected from their
measurements; additional caches and Source subtree reuse are not preselected.
HCL still has a reviewed fixed lexical/grammar fast path; portable TLA+ proof
policy and general recursive lowering have separate obligations.

* Cost and research discipline

Complete-call measurements include UTF-8 admission, scanning, recognition,
publication, encoding, C transfer and the consumer readback/release. History
priming and validation are outside timing. Instrumented stages are inclusive
and may nest; they are never summed as independent end-to-end costs.

Each engine refactor must document two questions with executable Scheme blocks:
why the author interface is preferable, and why the semantic/algorithmic
contract is justified. Grammar authors use hygienic syntax/rules and POO
composition or ordinary values as needed. Engine mechanics do not become new
cache, allocation or checkpoint clauses in the author DSL.

Paper claims must identify the implemented invariant and its assumptions.
Scanner suffix reuse does not inherit an optimal incremental-parser complexity
bound. Keep negative measurements and distinguish local receipts, remote CI,
syntax coverage and real downstream admission.
