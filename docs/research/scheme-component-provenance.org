#+title: Scheme component provenance: existing binding ownership and composition admission
#+date: 2026-10-06

* Question and scope

How should an author declaration, generated helper rules and POO composition
remain explainable after macro expansion and artifact generation? This study
extends [[file:scheme-list-role-composition.org][the multi-rule component experiment]]. It uses the current committed
baseline =783381f33fa1b309ac4cc4b0012696f24c852d93= plus local research fixtures.
The initial study left compiler code unchanged. The local implementation now
connects occurrence selection, canonical publication and a checked composed entry;
its current contracts and qualification are described below.

The objective is a coherent declaration-to-artifact contract. It is not to add
another grammar representation or a second parser implementation.

The earlier broad suggestion to design a new origin mapping needs refinement:
these existing inputs already support much of the work. A production design
still needs precise origin generation and composition association, but it should
start from the current compiler contracts.

Bound binding IDs are structured data: package, grammar, namespace and name.
The list fixture's =owner/tail= symbol spelling is only its deterministic helper
naming experiment; it is not a replacement binding namespace or a settled public
private-rule naming policy.

These checks distinguish recognition declarations, declaration provenance and
composition metadata. They do not prove invariance for arbitrary compiler
extensions or source transformations.

Source inspection adds a digest distinction: normalization's receipt
=grammarIrDigest= hashes the grammar body before =compositionDigest= is inserted;
Bound IR's =grammarDigest= hashes the canonical grammar value it receives.
These fields must not be treated as interchangeable hash names when connecting
receipts to artifacts. This distinction is source-inspected, rather than a new
receipt-hash assertion in the probe.

* Source-map construction constraints

The existing binding lookup chooses the first source-map section for a namespace.
If an explicit =rule= section only covers some names, a missing name falls back
to the module origin; it does not search a later auto-generated =rule= section.
The fixture therefore supplies all rule entries for this namespace. A production
collector must define merging before passing its final map to Bound IR.

Receipt role names alone cannot identify the selected reader occurrence. The
engine now captures source associations during accepted row operations, rather
than reconstructing them from receipt names. Its effective-source policy covers
equal merges, append, override, remove and reappend; repeated names retain
distinct step indices. The old append-only source map remains an explicit stale
negative control. See [[file:scheme-dsl-engine-admission.org::*Effective origins follow composition before binding][the selection contract]] for history and coordinate limits.

An author changes grammar semantics by changing declarations. Ordinary formatting
can change source locations and diagnostic identity without changing the grammar
meaning. This distinction matters for the earlier indentation discussion: source
metadata belongs in the explanatory binding product, while the target language's
recognition and layout policy remain explicit grammar decisions.

* Three composition-admission directions

| Direction | Strength | Remaining contract |
|-----------+----------+--------------------|
| Admit composed canonical IR through the existing language compiler owner | Retains its current composition field; avoids section reconstruction loss | Validate origin map, policy settings and receipt association together |
| Put composition association only beside Bound IR | Keeps explanatory data separate | Must define consistency with current Parser IR composition field and artifact admission |
| Persist an additional composition witness linked to the admitted products | Can retain detailed contribution history | Needs exact hash scopes, serialization and admission checks; do not invent it merely to duplicate the existing receipt |

Working preference: extend the existing language compilation boundary to accept
an already composed canonical declaration plus the established receipt and
binding inputs. Bind, compile and materialize through the same existing owners.
The current list-stage implementation uses this direct route. The separate
located-source reification study remains a historical control.

Canonical compilation and effective-source selection are locally implemented.
There is no new public author composition clause or additional artifact schema. Unknown policies or arbitrary
unverified metadata must not become a bypass of existing grammar admission.

* Relation to the papers and next discussion

Our interpretation of
[[https://www2.ccs.neu.edu/racket/pubs/icfp24-bgh.pdf][Ballantyne/Gamburg/Hemann]] is to keep an extensible author vocabulary and a
shared compilation core, with explicit boundaries that retain DSL invariants.
Our interpretation of
[[https://www2.ccs.neu.edu/racket/pubs/icfp10-cf.pdf][Culpepper/Felleisen]] is to explain mistakes in the author's vocabulary and
resolve declaration dependencies with the appropriate context. Neither paper
specifies our artifact hashes or component-origin schema.

The concrete Scheme/POO design now has evidence for contextual single-rule
macros, phase-qualified multi-rule construction, checked POO composition and
persisted declaration origins. The next discussion should settle the admission
contract for composed IR and provenance history before choosing a public author
surface. That will let syntax become simpler without making generated rules or
composition decisions opaque.

* Scheme design rationale: two required questions

This Scheme datum illustrates the research source-record shape. It is
metadata, not a new declaration API; its descriptive location is not a reader
line/column span.

#+begin_src scheme
'((path . "t/fixtures/language-pack-research/list-roles.ss")
  (location . (role call-arguments rule call-arguments/tail))
  (generated? . #t)
  (componentOwner . call-arguments))

;; Question 1: Why is this DSL design better?
;; Authors declare a component while generated helpers retain an explainable owner.
;; Concise syntax retains provenance, so diagnostics and tools need not guess origins.
;; Question 2: Why does this DSL design have a scientific basis?
;; Separate binding identity from provenance: source changes preserve bindingId/referenceDigest.
;; Evidence: 17 identity/artifact checks and 14 actual-language integration checks.
;; Falsifier: a source-only change alters reference identity or loaded Bound IR loses sources.
;; Limit: no public schema; reader-origin construction passes, actual-language loading times out.
#+end_src

Source integrity and accurate source attribution are separate obligations.
Persisting a supplied record does not prove that its origin was automatically
collected or that its history is correct.
