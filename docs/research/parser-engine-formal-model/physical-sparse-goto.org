#+title: Physical extraction and sparse goto composition

* Paper and implementation boundary

The closure and goto definitions in [[https://www.deinprogramm.de/sperber/papers/lr-essence-pepm.pdf][The Essence of LR Parsing]], Section 2.2,
are the semantic target. The implementation uses raw production records,
a dense core-symbol vector, interned symbol IDs and sparse bit masks. This
composition derives group meaning and layout bounds from those representations;
it does not require a final group-correctness certificate.

* Constructed representation

[[file:../../../formal/ParserEngine/PhysicalOperandExtraction.lean][PhysicalOperandExtraction]] projects actual operand base fields, discarding
annotations and actions. Induction over the native vector-write recurrence
proves every bounded read, including the untouched completed slot, equals the
constructed grammar's after-dot symbol. A fresh zero-filled vector is required.
[[file:../../../formal/ParserEngine/PhysicalCoreExtraction.lean][PhysicalCoreExtraction]] inverts arbitrary physical addresses and proves that
a symbol-bearing core advances to address plus one inside the same production.
Duplicate semantic productions retain separate physical addresses.

[[file:../../../formal/ParserEngine/PhysicalSymbolDiscovery.lean][PhysicalSymbolDiscovery]] derives the nonterminal discovery stream from the
physical vector. [[file:../../../formal/ParserEngine/SymbolDiscovery.lean][SymbolDiscovery]] separately constructs terminal and nonterminal
symbol IDs from an empty registry and proves index injectivity. Completed slots
contribute neither kind of symbol; terminal and nonterminal namespaces remain
distinct even when their numeric payloads agree.

* Sparse groups and publication

[[file:../../../formal/ParserEngine/SparseGroupMasks.lean][SparseGroupMasks]] follows accumulation, own/shared mask union and the
skip/merge recurrence. Its membership theorem identifies the origin of every
output bit in either the kernel or prediction items. [[file:../../../formal/ParserEngine/SparseGoto.lean][SparseGoto]] combines this
origin with physical symbol discovery and bounded advance. Every returned mask
is bounded without a caller-supplied bound; every decoded target is exactly a
paper goto-kernel item from the generated closure.

[[file:../../../formal/ParserEngine/KernelConstruction.lean][KernelConstruction]] starts at augmented physical core zero and derives
bounds for every successor in its construction history. Composing group meaning,
registry publication and the concrete closure factory gives the target closure
of each returned symbol/target pair. The theorem quantifies over arbitrary
grammars, histories, masks and published groups, rather than a fixture catalogue.

[[file:../../../formal/ParserEngine/SparseGroupSource.lean][SparseGroupSource]] independently describes the complete native symbol-catalog
and sparse-group declarations. The existing source extractor generates both
witnesses, and Lean rejects a declaration mismatch. Its checked adapter exposes
the proved mathematical group implementation only after schema equality.
Source equality identifies the supported procedure; it alone is not a proof of
arbitrary Scheme execution or of all mutable primitive behavior.

* Remaining universal correspondence

The set-level goto and kernel-bound obligations above are discharged. Universal
refinement of earliest-item sorting, mutable scratch reset, physical registry
storage and the complete native queue's control flow remains separate. In
particular, the sorted model preserves membership; this work does not claim an
arbitrary-host execution theorem for native list-sort. Existing native controls
compare complete row order, scratch reset and repeated calls against a full
closure traversal, but finite controls do not replace those universal proofs.
Host vector/hash primitives, allocation, GC and compiler behavior remain external
premises. Gambit is neither modified nor built. No performance claim follows.

in the axiom audit. Thirteen Scheme owners complete 262 cases with natural
